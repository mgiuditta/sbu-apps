title: Free is not spam
description: Apple rejected seven Sbu apps as spam. They do what other apps charge for, but for free. What happened, what I got wrong, and what I think of guideline 4.3.
date: 2026-10-20
---

Today Apple rejected Sbu Find, the app that helps you find lost headphones by following their Bluetooth signal. Reason: spam. According to the review, the app shares "a similar binary, metadata, and/or concept" with apps submitted by me "or other developers", "with only minor differences".

At the end of the message there is a warning: developers who keep submitting apps like this get longer reviews and risk removal from the developer program.

It is the seventh Sbu app rejected under the same guideline, 4.3, in six days.

## What happened

- **May and June 2026.** Sbu Scanner and Sbu Subscriptions are approved. Same brand, same promises, same shared code as today's apps.
- **14 October.** Six apps rejected in one evening: Level, Moon, Noise, Clean, Speed, Habits. Duplicates, variants in crowded categories, apps built from a template.
- **17 October.** I submit Find, alone, with answers to Apple's nine questions in the review notes: who wrote it, who it is for, what it shares with the others, no template, no third-party code.
- **20 October.** Find rejected as spam. The answers were not taken into account.

## What I got wrong

Part of the blame is mine. Six apps created in seven minutes and submitted on the same day look like an assembly line. The same six lines at the top of every description look like copy and paste. I changed both: one submission a week, and every description opens with what the app does.

Find went out alone, with its own description, for a job no other Sbu app does. It was rejected anyway.

## What I think

Find remakes, for free, Find Air, which charges from $4.99 a week for the same search. Find Air is on the App Store. There are dozens like it, nearly all with a subscription. The similar concept has been there for years, and as long as it costs money it is fine.

On every subscription and in-app purchase Apple keeps 15 to 30 percent. On a free app with no purchases it keeps nothing: it only gets the developer program's yearly fee.

I can't prove this is the reason. Apple doesn't say so, and I don't know what my reviewer thinks. But I see what the guideline does when applied like this: the app that costs $4.99 a week stays, the one that does the same for free is spam. A rule made against clones ends up protecting the apps that charge, and Apple with them.

Sbu exists for exactly this: take a simple job your phone can already do, one that someone charges you for every week, and remake it for free. No subscription, no in-app purchases, no account, no ads, no tracking. If that is spam, then yes, Sbu is spam.

## What now

I am not resubmitting Find. I asked Apple which apps they consider it similar to. I am not submitting other apps until they answer. If needed, I will take the case to the App Review Board.

The code of every Sbu app is public, on GitHub. Anyone can read it and decide for themselves whether it is a template.

If you made a free app and the same happened to you, write to me. I want to know whether this is my case or the rule.
