Thank you for the review. I believe Sbu Clean meets guidelines 4.3(a), 4.3(b) and 4.2.6, so I am not resubmitting. Here are the answers to the nine questions.

**1. What the app does and the problem it solves**

Sbu Clean frees space in the Photos library. It reads the library through PhotoKit, shows at the top how much space can be reclaimed and the iPhone's total and free storage, and then offers five ways to decide what to remove:

- Swipe review, month by month, most recent, random, "on this day", or one album at a time: swipe left to put a photo in the bin, right to keep it, with undo of the last choice. The position is saved, so the user resumes where they stopped, even across updates. Uncertain photos go to a "maybe" pile to decide later, or can be marked "always keep" so they are never proposed again.
- Duplicates and similar photos, found on the device with Vision feature prints (no image leaves the iPhone). In each group the app proposes the best photo to keep, judged by faces, sharpness, favourite status and whether it was edited, pre-selects the copies, and lets the user change the choice.
- Screenshots and screen recordings in a grid, selectable all at once.
- Videos sorted by size, to watch before deciding; a video can be compressed with AVFoundation to low, medium or high quality into a new copy, and the original stays until the user deletes it.
- Live Photos can be turned into still photos, saving the video part.

Nothing is deleted without the user reviewing the bin and confirming, after which iOS asks once more; the app tells the user that iOS keeps deleted photos for 30 days in Recently Deleted. A video that lives only in iCloud is downloaded only when the user asks for it. The app shows the space freed in total and in the last session, and a widget shows the iPhone's free space. There is no account, no server of mine and no network access.

**2. Intended user**

People whose iPhone or iCloud storage is full, often not technical, who want to get space back quickly and safely: parents with thousands of near-identical photos of children, people who screenshot everything, anyone who shoots long videos.

**3. Need or gap**

Photo-cleaning is a category where the top apps I studied charge a weekly or yearly subscription, limit the number of swipes per day on the free tier, or start a trial that converts to a subscription, and several analyse photos through their servers. Sbu Clean is free with nothing to buy, has no daily limit, no trial, no account, no ads and no analytics, does the duplicate analysis entirely on the device, keeps the original of every compressed video, has an explicit final review before anything is deleted, and is usable with VoiceOver and at the largest Dynamic Type sizes (the swipe card exposes keep, bin and undo as actions).

**4. Beta testing**

Testing was internal: TestFlight builds installed on a physical iPhone 15 Pro Max with a real library, worked through a written test checklist with exact pass/fail results (first launch and permission explanation, denied and limited access, each of the five modes, bin review and deletion, compression, Live Photo conversion, widget, largest text size, VoiceOver, light and dark, Italian and English, relaunch), plus the iPhone SE size in the simulator. I had no external beta testers for 1.0. Findings fixed before the production build:

- On a large library the scan stuttered and blocked the home screen. Fix: the scan was made incremental and the home updates smoothly while it runs.
- Videos stored only in iCloud were downloaded automatically during the scan, using data and time. Fix: such a video is downloaded only when the user opens or compresses it.
- The duplicate tests grouped differently on the device than in the simulator because Vision prints differ. Fix: the tests group with the device's own prints.
- The selection mark had a disk behind it that hid the photo; the month queue used different words from the home. Both fixed.

**5. Standalone or suite**

Sbu Clean is a standalone app. It is one of the apps I publish under the sbu.dev name, each doing one task in a different category: Sbu Clean (free up Photos library space), Sbu Habits (habit streaks), Sbu Level (level and clinometer), Sbu Moon (moon phase), Sbu Noise (sleep sounds), Sbu Speed (GPS speedometer), plus Sbu Scanner QR Barcode and Sbu Subscriptions, both already on the App Store since May and June 2026. None of them overlaps another in function: I never publish two apps in the same category. Sbu Clean is the only one that touches the Photos library and the only one that uses Vision.

**6. Could it be a feature of another app on my account**

No. No other app on my account has anything to do with photos or storage. Bundling it into another app would force every user of that app to be asked for full Photos library access, which I will not do. The apps are also free with no in-app purchases, so delivering this as a purchasable feature expansion is not possible.

**7. Code shared with other apps on my account**

Yes, a small Swift package of mine called SbuKit, about 1,700 lines, shared by all my apps. It contains the colour and typography tokens of my design system, button styles, the large-number component, the "no subscription / no in-app purchases / no account / no ads / no tracking / data on the device" pills, and the Settings screen with the privacy policy and the font licence. It holds no photo, Vision or business logic. Sbu Clean's own code is about 6,200 lines: the library scan, the swipe queues and their saved positions, duplicate grouping and best-photo choice, compression, Live Photo conversion, the bin, the widget and the tests.

**8. Code shared with third-party apps**

None. The app uses only Apple frameworks (SwiftUI, PhotoKit, Vision, AVFoundation, WidgetKit). The only outside asset is the Bricolage Grotesque typeface (SIL Open Font License), used for the large number and credited in Settings.

No. I am Matteo Giuditta, an individual developer; I designed and wrote Sbu Clean myself in Swift and SwiftUI, in Xcode, in my own repository. No commercial template, app generation service or third party provided its code, content, concept or branding. sbu.dev is my own site.
