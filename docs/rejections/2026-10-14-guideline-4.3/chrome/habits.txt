Thanks for reviewing Sbu Habits. I think it meets 4.3 and 4.2.6, so rather than resubmit I'll answer the nine questions.

1. A habit tracker built around one number, the "full streak": days in a row on which every habit due got done. Up to 24 habits, yes/no or counted ("8 glasses of water"), daily, on weekdays, N per week or month, or on a single day; you mark one by holding its ring. Most of the code handles what breaks streaks in real life. Habits to avoid have a daily limit (0 for "no smoking"). Timed habits run a Live Activity countdown and mark themselves at the end. A habit can read Apple Health (steps, water, sleep, Activity rings and others) and mark itself when the day's total hits the goal, even in the background. Daily notes, "skip today" for sick days, a custom start of day for night shifts, reminders only on due days when not yet done. Interactive widgets, an Apple Watch app with a complication, statistics, JSON export/import. Off by default until the user turns them on: sync through their own iCloud, and read-only sharing of one habit. No account, no server on my side.

2. People building or dropping a daily routine who want one honest number: shift workers, people who already log activity in Health, people with a habit to cut down, Watch owners.

3. The habit apps I looked at sell Health linking, Watch support, timers and sharing as paid tiers, and most want an account. Sbu Habits is free with nothing to buy, no account, no ads, no analytics, reads Health without writing to it, keeps iCloud opt-in, and treats habits to avoid and timed habits as real types, not add-ons. Works with VoiceOver and the biggest Dynamic Type sizes.

4. Internal. Five TestFlight builds on my iPhone 15 Pro Max, through a written pass/fail checklist: first launch, add/mark/undo/skip, templates, notes, widgets, largest text, VoiceOver, light and dark, Italian and English, export/import, relaunch; iPhone SE in the simulator. Timed habits, Health, iCloud and the Watch app have automated tests and simulator runs, but I haven't lived with them on a device for days yet. No outside testers for 1.0. Fixed because of this testing: a UI pass turned up ten defects; at the largest text sizes the add form overlapped its button and words broke mid-way (issue #439); the play button swallowed small rings; on a short iPhone a note's last line hid behind the keyboard.

5. Standalone. I publish a handful of apps as sbu.dev, each for one job in its own category: Habits (streaks), Level (level and clinometer), Moon (moon phase), Noise (sleep sounds), Speed (GPS speedometer), Clean (freeing Photos space), and two on the store since May and June 2026, Sbu Scanner QR Barcode and Sbu Subscriptions. Never two in one category. Habits alone tracks anything across days, touches Health, or has a Watch app.

6. No. The nearest, Sbu Subscriptions, is about recurring payments; nothing in it applies to habits, and merging would drag Health and Watch permissions into a money app. All my apps are free with no in-app purchases, so a paid feature expansion isn't an option either.

7. Yes, one package: SbuKit, which I wrote and every app of mine imports. About 1,700 lines of design system: colour and type tokens, button styles, the big-number view, the "no subscription / no account / no ads / no tracking" pills, and a Settings screen with privacy policy and font licence. No habit, Health, sync or business logic in it. Habits itself is about 14,600 lines.

8. None. Apple frameworks only: SwiftUI, SwiftData, HealthKit, WidgetKit, ActivityKit, WatchConnectivity, CloudKit, UserNotifications. The one outside asset is the Bricolage Grotesque typeface (SIL OFL), credited in Settings.

9. No client or partner. I'm Matteo Giuditta, an individual developer. I designed and wrote the app in Swift and SwiftUI, in my own repository. No template, no app generation service, nobody else's content or branding. sbu.dev is my site.
