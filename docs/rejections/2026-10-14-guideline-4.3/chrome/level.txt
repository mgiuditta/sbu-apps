Thank you for the review. I believe Sbu Level meets 4.3(a), 4.3(b) and 4.2.6, so I am not resubmitting. Answers to the nine questions:

1. Sbu Level is a spirit level and clinometer on the iPhone's motion sensors (Core Motion gravity vector). Flat, it shows a bubble level. Stood on any of its four edges, it shows that edge's tilt from 0° to 90° to a tenth of a degree, on a dial and as a large number. The reading can be locked, the screen kept on, and a haptic tap marks exactly 0.0° or 90.0°. The problem it solves is calibration on a surface that is not level: a one-tap "zero here" bakes the table's error into the phone, so Sbu Level calibrates each side with two readings, the phone turned 180° between them, which cancels the surface error and keeps only the sensor offset. Implausible calibrations are refused, each side resets separately. Only these offsets are stored, on the device. No permissions, no network.

2. People who hang shelves, pictures and rails, check whether a table, washing machine or bed is level, or need a quick angle for a ramp, a roof pitch or a saw cut, without carrying a physical level. Also VoiceOver users: the angle is read aloud.

3. The level apps I studied are paid up front, carry ads, or put the clinometer or calibration behind a subscription, and most calibrate with a single reading that only works on a level surface. Sbu Level is free with nothing to buy, no ads, no account, no network, calibrates correctly on a tilted surface, measures on all four edges, and is usable with VoiceOver and the largest Dynamic Type sizes.

4. Internal: TestFlight builds on a physical iPhone 15 Pro Max, worked through a written pass/fail checklist with exact expected values (flat, each edge, 0° to 90° in one movement, a 45° set square, a door jamb, lock, calibration and reset, largest text, VoiceOver, light/dark, Italian/English, relaunch) plus iPhone SE in the simulator. No external testers for 1.0. Fixed before this build: a fresh install read 9.2° on a flat table from a stored calibration, now discarded and implausible values refused; turning from 0° to 90° made the reading dip (60° read 30°), dial maths corrected; on the iPhone SE the calibration sheet's "Measure" button was below the fold, sheet reduced to one section; the needle hid the dial marks.

5. Standalone. It is one of the apps I publish under the sbu.dev name, each doing one task in a different category: Level (level and clinometer), Habits (habit streaks), Moon (moon phase), Noise (sleep sounds), Speed (GPS speedometer), Clean (free up Photos space), plus Sbu Scanner QR Barcode and Sbu Subscriptions, on the App Store since May and June 2026. I never publish two apps in the same category. Level is the only one that uses the motion sensors or measures an angle.

6. No. No other app of mine measures anything physical, and none shares a user need with a level. All my apps are free with no in-app purchases, so a paid feature expansion is not possible.

No sensor or business logic. Level's own code is about 1,400 lines: motion reading, edge detection, the dial, the two-reading calibration, settings and tests.
