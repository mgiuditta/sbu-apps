# Sbu Noise 1.0 (build 3), reply to Guidelines 4.3 and 4.2.6

Thank you for the review. I believe Sbu Noise meets 4.3(a), 4.3(b) and 4.2.6, so I am not resubmitting. Answers to the nine questions:

1. Sbu Noise plays a sound to fall asleep to. It bundles 34 looping sounds: coloured noises generated by the app and public-domain field recordings of rain, water, nature, home and travel, normalised to the same loudness and looped without a seam. Nothing is streamed. The user picks a sound, marks favourites, hides or reorders sounds, and builds mixes of up to five sounds with individual volume, placed around the listener in headphones. A sleep timer fades out and can repeat on chosen days; an alarm fades a sound in with snooze, backed by a system alarm (AlarmKit on iOS 26, Time Sensitive notifications before) that rings only if the app is not running. A bedside clock with adjustable brightness. Playback continues in the background and locked, with AirPlay and headphone controls, and starts or stops from a widget, a Control Center control, Shortcuts and Siri. No account, no server, no network.

2. People who sleep badly or in a noisy place, shift workers sleeping by day, parents settling a baby, people with tinnitus, people who work with headphones.

3. The sleep-sound apps I studied stream their catalogue (network, often an account), lock most sounds, mixes or the alarm behind a subscription, or carry ads. Sbu Noise is free with nothing to buy, works offline, has no account, no ads, no analytics, offers spatial mixes, an alarm with a real system backup, Control Center and Siri, and equal loudness so switching sounds at night never startles. Usable with VoiceOver and the largest Dynamic Type sizes.

4. Internal: TestFlight builds on a physical iPhone 15 Pro Max, with and without headphones, worked through a written pass/fail checklist (every sound and group, favourites, hide and reorder, mixes, sleep timer, alarm with the app killed, bedside clock, background and lock-screen playback, widget and Control Center, largest text, VoiceOver, light/dark, Italian/English, relaunch) plus iPhone SE in the simulator. No external testers for 1.0. Fixed before this build: sounds differed widely in loudness, now all at -18 LUFS; several recordings clicked at the loop point, now the loop wraps through decoded padding; the train recording had station beeps, trimmed.

5. Standalone. It is one of the apps I publish under the sbu.dev name, each doing one task in a different category: Noise (sleep sounds), Habits (habit streaks), Level (level and clinometer), Moon (moon phase), Speed (GPS speedometer), Clean (free up Photos space), plus Sbu Scanner QR Barcode and Sbu Subscriptions, on the App Store since May and June 2026. I never publish two apps in the same category. Noise is the only one that plays audio or has the background audio mode.

6. No. No other app of mine plays sound or concerns sleep; bundling 34 sounds and background audio into, say, a habit tracker would bloat it for everyone. All my apps are free with no in-app purchases, so a paid feature expansion is not possible.

7. Yes: SbuKit, my own Swift package, about 1,700 lines shared by all my apps: colour and type tokens, button styles, the large-number component, the "no subscription / no account / no ads / no tracking" pills, and the Settings screen with privacy policy and font licence. No audio or business logic. Noise's own code is about 5,200 lines.

8. None. Only Apple frameworks (SwiftUI, AVFoundation, AlarmKit, WidgetKit, App Intents, MediaPlayer). Outside assets: the field recordings, CC0 and credited one by one in Settings > Sound Credits, and the Bricolage Grotesque typeface (SIL OFL).

9. No. I am Matteo Giuditta, an individual developer; I designed and wrote the app myself in Swift and SwiftUI, in my own repository. No template, app generation service or third party provided code, content, concept or branding. sbu.dev is my site.
