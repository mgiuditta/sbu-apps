# Sbu Noise 1.0 (build 3), reply to Guidelines 4.3 and 4.2.6

Thank you for the review. I believe Sbu Noise meets guidelines 4.3(a), 4.3(b) and 4.2.6, so I am not resubmitting. Here are the answers to the nine questions.

**1. What the app does and the problem it solves**

Sbu Noise plays a sound to fall asleep to. It bundles 34 looping sounds: coloured noises (white, pink, brown and others) generated by the app, and public-domain field recordings of rain, water, nature, home and travel, all normalised to the same loudness and looped without an audible seam. Everything is in the app; it never streams or downloads.

The user picks a sound, marks favourites, hides or reorders sounds, and can build a mix of up to five sounds, each with its own volume and placed around the listener in headphones. A sleep timer fades the sound out and can repeat on chosen days; an alarm fades a chosen sound in at a set time, with snooze, backed by a system alarm (AlarmKit on iOS 26 and later, Time Sensitive notifications before) that rings only if the app is not running at that time. A bedside clock with adjustable brightness is one tap away. Playback continues in the background and with the screen locked, works with AirPlay and the headphone controls, and can be started and stopped from a Home Screen widget, a Control Center control, Shortcuts and Siri. There is no account, no server of mine and no network access.

**2. Intended user**

People who sleep badly or in a noisy place, shift workers sleeping by day, parents settling a baby, people with tinnitus who use masking noise, and people who work or study with headphones on.

**3. Need or gap**

The sleep-sound apps I studied before building this one stream their catalogue (so they need the network and often an account), lock most sounds, mixes or the alarm behind a subscription, or carry ads. Sbu Noise is free with nothing to buy, works entirely offline, has no account, no ads and no analytics, offers mixes with spatial placement, an alarm with a real system backup, a Control Center control and Siri, and keeps every sound at equal loudness so switching sounds at night never startles. It is usable with VoiceOver (the player reads the sound, position in the group and each control) and at the largest Dynamic Type sizes.

**4. Beta testing**

Testing was internal: TestFlight builds installed on a physical iPhone 15 Pro Max, listened to with and without headphones, and worked through a written test checklist with exact pass/fail results (every sound and group, favourites, hide and reorder, mixes, sleep timer, alarm with the app killed, bedside clock, background and lock-screen playback, widget and Control Center, largest text size, VoiceOver, light and dark, Italian and English, relaunch), plus the iPhone SE size in the simulator. I had no external beta testers for 1.0. Findings fixed before the production build:

- Sounds differed widely in loudness, so switching from rain to a fan was a jump. Fix: every sound was normalised to -18 LUFS.
- Several recordings had an audible click at the loop point. Fix: the loop now wraps through the decoded padding, so the seam is inaudible.
- The train recording contained station beeps that woke the listener. Fix: the recording was trimmed to the window section.
- The store screenshots showed an idle player; the test pass asked for a sound playing. Fix: screenshots regenerated with a sound playing and a real title.

**5. Standalone or suite**

Sbu Noise is a standalone app. It is one of the apps I publish under the sbu.dev name, each doing one task in a different category: Sbu Noise (sleep sounds), Sbu Habits (habit streaks), Sbu Level (level and clinometer), Sbu Moon (moon phase), Sbu Speed (GPS speedometer), Sbu Clean (free up Photos library space), plus Sbu Scanner QR Barcode and Sbu Subscriptions, both already on the App Store since May and June 2026. None of them overlaps another in function: I never publish two apps in the same category. Sbu Noise is the only one that plays audio and the only one with the background audio mode.

**6. Could it be a feature of another app on my account**

No. No other app on my account plays sound or has anything to do with sleep. Bundling 34 sounds and the background audio mode into, say, a habit tracker would bloat it for every user who never uses them. The apps are also free with no in-app purchases, so delivering this as a purchasable feature expansion is not possible.

**7. Code shared with other apps on my account**

Yes, a small Swift package of mine called SbuKit, about 1,700 lines, shared by all my apps. It contains the colour and typography tokens of my design system, button styles, the large-number component, the "no subscription / no in-app purchases / no account / no ads / no tracking / data on the device" pills, and the Settings screen with the privacy policy and the font licence. It holds no audio or business logic. Sbu Noise's own code is about 5,200 lines: the catalogue, the audio engine and noise generators, mixes and spatial placement, timers and alarms, the bedside clock, the widget, the Control Center control, App Intents and the tests.

**8. Code shared with third-party apps**

None. The app uses only Apple frameworks (SwiftUI, AVFoundation, AlarmKit, WidgetKit, App Intents, MediaPlayer). The outside assets are the field recordings, released under CC0 and credited one by one in Settings > Sound Credits, and the Bricolage Grotesque typeface (SIL Open Font License), credited in Settings.

No. I am Matteo Giuditta, an individual developer; I designed and wrote Sbu Noise myself in Swift and SwiftUI, in Xcode, in my own repository. No commercial template, app generation service or third party provided its code, content, concept or branding. sbu.dev is my own site.
